What remodeling in Cheviot Hills actually involves

Cheviot Hills was founded in 1924, and the residential section west of Motor Avenue was built up through the 1920s. East of Motor Avenue and south of Forrester Drive, much of the neighborhood sits on the former California Country Club, where the houses date to the early 1950s. The neighborhood is framed by the Rancho Park Golf Course and Hillcrest Country Club to the north and Manning Avenue to the southwest, with Century City, Beverlywood, Castle Heights and Palms around it.

The 1920s houses have plaster walls, original windows and period detail worth keeping. The early-1950s houses have simpler framing and are generally easier to open up and add onto. Values have climbed to the point where many original houses have been torn down for new construction. A remodel is the alternative, and it only makes sense when it is planned well: keep what makes the house worth saving, and replace what is past its life.

Permits & Local Rules

Who approves your project in Cheviot Hills

Cheviot Hills is a City of Los Angeles neighborhood, so permits go through LADBS. The city's floor-area limits for single-family lots shape every addition, and several homeowners' associations cover parts of the area. We check the parcel and any private restrictions before design.

  • LADBS permits and inspections for structural, plumbing, electrical and mechanical work. Pulled in our name under License #1008892, never in yours.
  • Floor-area limits shape additions. The City of LA caps residential floor area on single-family lots. We confirm the remaining allowance on your lot before anyone designs a second story or a rear addition.
  • Homeowners' associations cover parts of the area, including the Cheviot Hills Homeowners Association and separate associations in Castle Heights and California Country Club Estates. We check for private restrictions on your tract.
  • Pre-1978 homes require lead-safe work practices, which covers essentially every original house here. We test before demolition or sanding begins.
  • Seismic retrofits fit naturally into a remodel. Older raised-foundation houses often lack foundation bolting or cripple-wall bracing. When the floors or walls are open, adding it costs far less than doing it later.

Local Experience

Three things we plan for on every Cheviot Hills job

01

Plaster walls need a plaster plan

Original 1920s houses have plaster on lath, not drywall. Cutting, patching and matching it takes different methods and different trades, and a bad patch stays visible. We plan plaster repair as its own line item rather than pricing it as drywall.

02

The 1950s houses open up well

The early-1950s homes east of Motor Avenue tend to have simpler framing and fewer original details to protect. That often makes them the better candidates for opening walls, reworking layouts and adding space, and we design to that strength.

03

New construction next door affects your job

New houses on neighboring lots are common here. That can mean shared staging, competing parking, and a neighbor's work changing drainage or shared fences. We coordinate with adjacent sites and photograph conditions at the property line before we start.

Should I remodel or tear down my Cheviot Hills house?

It depends on the foundation, the structure, the condition of the systems and how much floor area your lot still allows. We assess those first and give you a written comparison of what a remodel delivers against what a rebuild requires, so you can decide before paying for design.

Can I add a second story in Cheviot Hills?

Often, within the floor-area limits the City of LA sets for single-family lots. The existing foundation usually needs engineering review to carry a second floor. We confirm the remaining allowance and the structural path before design starts.
