El Segundo, California

What remodeling in El Segundo actually involves

El Segundo is its own city with its own building department, and its housing stock is older and tighter than most of the South Bay - 1920s through 1950s homes on compact lots, many of them still close to their original footprint. Lot coverage, setbacks and existing garage placement do more to determine what is possible here than budget does.

That makes the feasibility conversation the important one. Before anyone draws a plan we check what the lot will actually carry: whether the addition fits, whether the garage can convert without losing required parking, whether going up is cheaper than going out. Homeowners here are frequently told yes by a designer and no by the city. We would rather have that conversation on the first visit, for free.

Permits & Local Rules

Who approves your project in El Segundo

El Segundo issues its own building permits through the city’s Building and Safety division - not LA City or LA County. It is a small department that reviews carefully, and plans that ignore lot coverage or parking requirements come back quickly.

  • City of El Segundo plan check and inspections - a separate submittal process from anything in Los Angeles.
  • Lot coverage and setbacks are the binding constraint on most projects. Feasibility gets checked before design, not after.
  • Garage conversions have to account for required parking. Losing the garage without a compliant plan is the most common reason an El Segundo ADU stalls.
  • Pre-1978 homes need lead-safe practices, and a good share of El Segundo predates that. We test rather than assume.

Common Questions

El Segundo FAQs

Who issues building permits in El Segundo?

The City of El Segundo does, through its own Building and Safety division - El Segundo is an independent city, so LADBS and LA County have no role. We submit to the city, meet its inspectors and carry California Contractor License #1008892.

Can I convert my El Segundo garage into an ADU?

Often, but the parking and lot-coverage rules matter more here than in most cities because the lots are small. Before you pay for plans we check what the parcel allows and whether converting the garage creates a parking problem the city will not approve. If it does not pencil, we say so.

Is an addition or a second storey better on a small El Segundo lot?

It depends on your coverage numbers. When a ground-floor addition would push the lot past allowable coverage, building up is frequently the only permittable option - and occasionally the cheaper one once you account for foundation and hardscape work. We price both before recommending either.
